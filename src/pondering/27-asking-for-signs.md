# Asking for Signs?

Yesterday, I was asked an interesting question: What is my view on Gideon asking God for signs and Thomas’ unbelief? 

I had never really deeply thought about it before, but as I reflected on it, I realized that the two situations are quite different. Gideon was certainly fearful and uncertain, but he brought his fear and uncertainty to God. He was asking God for confirmation. Thomas, on the other hand, expressed his doubt to the other disciples and essentially said that unless he saw Jesus for himself, he would not believe.

Both were looking for something tangible. The difference was where they brought their doubt: Gideon brought his uncertainty to God, while Thomas declared his unbelief to others.

And the outcomes are also strikingly different. Gideon, despite his fear, was still willing to seek God and was eventually used by God as a judge to deliver Israel. Thomas, however, was confronted by Jesus.

This also deepen my thought about asking God for signs. 

When I was small, I used to think, “I don't want to be fearful like Gideon, so I shouldn't ask God for signs.” 

But as I grew older, I began to understand that when we are afraid, uncertain, or struggling with faith, we should not simply suppress those doubts. We should bring them to God. There is a difference between demanding a sign from a posture of unbelief, and, humbly asking God for confirmation because we are struggling to trust Him. 

And now I realize that, in a sense, we are always asking for a “sign.” Yes, the ultimate “sign” is FAITH itself. Even if God gives us some kind of physical manifestation, tangible confirmation, or even a Scripture that speaks directly to our situation, we still need faith in God to perceive it correctly, to interpret it rightly, and to recognize what God is doing through it. 

After having this thought, Hebrews 11:1 immediately came to mind:
“Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.”

It was almost as if Scripture had already given the conclusion to what I had been contemplating. We may ask for signs because we want to see something, but faith ultimately concerns what is not yet seen.

The ultimate question is not simply whether we ask God for a sign, but where we bring our doubts and what we do with them. Do we take them to God, or do we allow them to grow to greater unbelief?

Faith is not necessarily the absence of doubt, but the willingness to bring even our doubt to God and ask Him to strengthen our faith. And ultimately, we cannot manufacture that faith in God within ourselves, because Jesus is the only author and finisher of our faith in Him. We can bring even our imperfect faith to Him and ask Him to perfect it.
