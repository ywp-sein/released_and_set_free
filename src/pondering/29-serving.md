# Serving

Weeks ago, I was asked a very interesting question at church:

**If the pastor or leader of a church is not well equipped theologically, but is passionate and genuinely loves the Lord, and the church itself is not yet spiritually mature, would you still go to that church?**

It is an interesting question because, for a long time, I have heard people say that they want to go to a certain church because the church satisfies their needs. I have even encountered people recommending their church to me because, as they said, “This church satisfies a lot of my needs.”

Together with the question I was asked, I think there is another question underneath it:

**Are we willing to serve, especially when we see a need?**

Because if the pastor does not have a strong theological background, that is a need. If the church is not yet spiritually mature, that is also a need.

I think the answer is always: **Where does God want us to go?**

Of course, we need to be fed spiritually. We need shepherds and leaders who can teach us, and brothers and sisters who can encourage us. God can also guide us in a practical way. Maybe He brings us to a place where we can first receive some spiritual food, so that we have the strength to serve.

But I think we should have another attitude: **we should serve from the perspective that we are not lacking, because God is our provider.**

If this is really true for us, then maybe the question becomes less about, *“What can I get from this church?”* and more about, *“How can I serve?”*

Of course, sometimes we will lack wisdom. But James tells us to ask God for wisdom, and He will give it to us generosely. Sometimes we may lack strength or capacity. But God is with us in our needs, and He is the one who provides.

So if our starting point is simply, **“I want to serve, because I know God will provide,”** then it changes a lot.

Sometimes we think about our schedules, our capacity, or other things, and these things draw us back from serving. And sometimes that is right. Sometimes God really does want us to put our priority somewhere else.

But I think we also need to review our attitude toward serving.

If we don't really want to serve, then maybe we should not serve. Otherwise, we may just be offering something like profane fire—serving because we want to fit everything into our schedule, get people to do their tasks, check the list, and eventually miss the point of serving.

I think the best way of serving is to come to God with the understanding that **we have nothing except ourselves and our hearts.**

All the good things come from Him. All the power comes from Him. And only when we acknowledge that He is the source of our power to do good can His power flow through us without blockage.

## We Don't Need to Be Needed

There is also another perspective that we need to be careful about.

Sometimes we think, **“This place needs me.”**

It sounds like serving, and sometimes it really is. We see a need and genuinely want to help.

But sometimes, underneath that thought, there is another need: **we want to be needed.**

We want to feel important. We want to feel that this church needs us, that without us something cannot be done.

But that can also become a need that we are trying to satisfy through serving.

So I think there is a difference between genuinely helping and serving because we need to feel needed.

If we really understand that God is our provider, then we don't have to prove our importance through serving.

And that also means that **it is okay to be rejected.**

Maybe we offer to help, but they don't need our help.

Maybe we want to serve in a certain way, but someone else is chosen.

Maybe we see a need, but God doesn't actually want us to be the one who fills it.

And that should be okay.

Because we are not serving in order to prove that we are useful or important. We are serving because we belong to God.

So we can offer ourselves, and if the answer is no, I believe God will still use this preparation in His plan.

## It Is Not About the Number of People

I also think about the story of Gideon.

God actually reduced Gideon's army. It was not about having a large number of people. God wanted Israel to know that the victory came from Him, not from their own strength.

I think this is also important when we think about serving in the church.

Sometimes we look at a church and think, *“There are not enough people. Who is going to do all these things? How can we possibly serve if there are so few people?”*

But maybe we don't need to be so afraid.

**It is not ultimately about the number of people.**

God can work through a small number of people, plus, angels are also serving us. He can provide what is needed. Of course, we still need wisdom, and we should not ignore practical limitations or expect a few people to do everything.

But we don't need to be afraid that there are not enough people.

Instead, we can ask:

**“Lord, what do You want me to do?”**

And then we trust Him with the rest.

## We Live for Him

I think this is really about our attitude toward serving.

We no longer live for ourselves. **We live for Him and for His church.**

And He is our provider.

So we don't have to serve from a place of fear or lack. We don't have to think that everything depends on us.

We can simply come to God and say:

**“Lord, You know what I need. You know what this church needs. You are the provider. Show me how You want me to serve, and give me what I need.”**

Maybe the question is not simply, *“What can this church give me?”*

Maybe the question is:

**“Where does God want me to go, and how does He want me to serve?”**

Because if God is truly our provider, then we can serve without fear.

We can serve without needing to be needed.

We can even serve while being okay with rejection.

We can offer our time, our hands, our hearts, and whatever little we have—and trust God to do what only He can do.

**Because the church does not ultimately depend on us. It always depends on Him.**
