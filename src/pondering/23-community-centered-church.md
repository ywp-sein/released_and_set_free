# Community-centered "Church?"

Instead of being united through the Gospel and the Holy Spirit, a community-centered "church?" is united by common psychological needs and, at times, even physical needs.

Because the Gospel is not at the center, there is no power to renew people inwardly. As a result, their psychological needs are never fundamentally addressed.

Desires without boundaries will always expand. In the long run, people's psychological needs continue to grow, and their visions begin to diverge, moving further away from the community's original, unspoken constitution—the shared needs that brought them together. Consequently, rather than discussing how to serve others more faithfully and reach outward, the primary concern becomes whose needs are more important.

What is even more troubling is that many sermons and teachings today speak directly to people's psychological needs and then redirect them toward personal ambition, making listeners feel moved and encouraged. Yet this is not the conviction of the Holy Spirit; it is self-fulfillment packaged as a TED Talk. (I am not referring to preachers becoming emotional during a sermon, but to the substance of the message itself.)

Sadly, some "churches?" today follow this model, setting goals that ultimately serve self-fulfillment. Those goals may be achieved, and the congregation may grow in number, but people's hearts remain unchanged. Whether they embrace a prosperity gospel, a poverty gospel, or some other variation, what they ultimately believe is a false gospel of self-fulfillment rather than the Gospel itself.

For if the Gospel is not at the center, there will be shame, unforgiveness, woundedness, and emptiness. But if the Gospel is at the center, there will be true repentance, genuine forgiveness, miraculous healing, and lasting satisfaction.
