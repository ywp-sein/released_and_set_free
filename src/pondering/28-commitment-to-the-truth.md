# Commitment to the Truth: Why Is There Still Struggle?

Jesus said, **“You will know the truth, and the truth will set you free”** (John 8:32).

Yet if the Truth sets us free, why do we still struggle?

There is a misconception that once we experience struggle, it means we are no longer moving toward freedom. But what may actually be happening is that, when we begin to embrace the Truth, there is a counterforce from the flesh. This struggle can itself be an expression of His grace and mercy, exposing the things we have been desperately trying to hold on to because of our fleshly desires.

What feels like resistance is actually God revealing what still needs to be surrendered.

Only when we loosen our grip on those fleshly desires can we eventually taste freedom in that particular area.

To experience that freedom, we need to repent.

Repentance is more than simply feeling sorry for what we have done. We can weep over the same sin a hundred times and still return to it for the hundred-and-first time. Sorrow may acknowledge our failure, but repentance turns the heart back toward God.

Repentance is acknowledging that our judgment about something is wrong, and that God's judgment and standard are right. It is surrendering our own understanding and submitting ourselves to His Truth.

As God renews our minds and gives us a new understanding of what is right, we are then called to put that renewed understanding into practice—to tame our tongues, discipline our behaviors, and bring our thoughts and desires into submission to His will.

Repentance, therefore, is not simply a change of emotion. It is a turning of the mind, the heart, and ultimately the life.

And this is where repentance begins to bear fruit.

When we truly act upon the convictions God has renewed within us, our repentance is no longer merely something we profess with our lips; it becomes something demonstrated through our lives.

We may still struggle. We may still stumble. But the direction has changed.

We are no longer defending the sin we once justified. We are learning to surrender it.

We are no longer insisting that our judgment is right. We are learning to trust that His is.

We are no longer merely feeling sorry for our disobedience. We are learning to walk in obedience.

Perhaps, then, the struggle is not always evidence that we are moving away from freedom.

Sometimes, **the struggle is where God reveals what is still holding us captive, so that by His grace, we can finally let it go.**

And as we surrender, repent, and walk in obedience, we begin to experience the freedom that Christ has promised.
